# Run Whisper Turbo Locally for Greek Meetings

**TLDR: build step 1 on Whisper large-v3-turbo, run locally with mlx-whisper inside WhisperLiveKit and forced to Greek. Keep NVIDIA Canary-1B-v2 as the accuracy challenger to test. Treat Parakeet v3 only as the fast fallback.** The ranked top 3 is: **#1 Whisper large-v3-turbo**. It is free, private and mature on Mac, gets about 14% WER on clean Greek read speech and about 15% on real Greek meeting-style audio, and already has a live server with speaker labels. **#2 Canary-1B-v2** has the best published open-model Greek score (9.21% WER), but it comes from NVIDIA's own table, and nobody has shown it running live on a Mac. **#3 Parakeet TDT 0.6b v3** is very fast and native on Apple Silicon, but third parties measure about 36% Greek WER, so roughly one word in three is wrong. As a quality ceiling, the best independent real-world Greek test found cloud (ElevenLabs Scribe v2) only **about 2 WER points better** than Whisper: 13% vs 15%. Local is therefore "close enough" for meetings, and the privacy win is large. Two caveats come first. **No source measures any of these models on an M1 Pro**, so every speed number for your Mac below is an estimate. And **every model struggles with Greek mixed with English tech words**, which is normal in Greek work meetings. Whether Mistral Voxtral supports Greek is disputed between sources (see the cloud section). Do not plan on Voxtral.

## Whisper holds 11–14% on clean Greek and about 15–20% in real meetings

Greek WER numbers can only be compared within one table, because scoring rules (whether accents and tonos are stripped, how numbers are written) change the result a lot. The same model, Whisper-large-v3 on the same FLEURS Greek test, scores **11.02%** in an Athena RC/ILSP paper ([Paraskevopoulos et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.15284v1)) and **27.03%** in NVIDIA's Canary paper ([Sekoyan et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14128v2)). Neither paper explains the gap. Within the ILSP table the order is clear. On FLEURS / Common Voice Greek, large-v3 scores **11.02 / 13.98**, large-v2 12.95 / 16.03, medium 19.45 / 21.30 and small 33.99 / 33.37. Small and medium are therefore not serious options. On a Mac specifically, an independent test of about 150 FLEURS Greek sentences on an M5 MacBook Air (32 GB) measured **Whisper large-v3-turbo at 13.94% WER** with no failed outputs, while Qwen3-ASR-1.7B scored 30.22% ([whispernotes.app](https://whispernotes.app/blog/qwen3-asr-vs-whisper)).

Clean read speech flatters every model. A Greek production team (KIEFER SA / Sophea AI Lab, Athens) reports **15.73% WER on Greek business meetings** even for its fine-tuned Whisper, and 18.27% for its fine-tuned Canary. It also finds Turbo close to Large on clean speech but clearly behind on informal audio: 20.65 vs 15.97 on its real-world aggregate ([arXiv 2609.13498](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13498)). That gives a practical rule: **use turbo for the live captions, then re-run full large-v3 on the recording after the meeting** if you need the cleanest transcript. **Estimate, not measured: expect 15–20% WER on your own meetings**, not the 11–14% FLEURS figures.

Canary's 9.21% is the strongest open number on paper. It sits in a table where Whisper scored an unusually bad 27.03%, so the size of Canary's lead is probably overstated ([Sekoyan et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14128v2)). On business meetings, the KIEFER results above put fine-tuned Canary *behind* fine-tuned Whisper. Canary is still worth a test, but the evidence does not support switching to it yet. Parakeet v3 shows how unreliable vendor numbers can be. NVIDIA's model card says **20.7%** Greek FLEURS ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3)). FluidInference measured **36.9%** over 650 FLEURS Greek files on an Apple M4 Pro, its second-worst language of 25 ([FluidInference](https://docs.fluidinference.com/reference/benchmarks)), and Spokenly measured 35.71% ([Spokenly](https://spokenly.app/blog/parakeet-models)).

Other options fall away for clear reasons. Qwen3-ASR supports Greek but is weak: **28.08% (1.7B) and 49.67% (0.6B)** official FLEURS WER ([Qwen3-ASR report](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21337)). Meta Omnilingual 7B scored 14.50 against Whisper's 13.58 in the same BuzzASR table ([arXiv 2609.09554](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09554v2)). It needs about 17 GiB of memory and only accepts clips under 40 seconds ([model card](https://huggingface.co/facebook/omniASR-LLM-7B/commit/4b0bada258b398cb7e6c5b3a6ed5448fb914385b)), so it is heavier than Whisper and no more accurate. Greek fine-tunes do not beat stock large-v3 on public tests either: the best one, BuzzASR/greek (MIT), gets 11.0 on FLEURS ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/BuzzASR/greek)), and OpenCouncil found an earlier Greek-specialised open fine-tune **about 3x worse than plain Whisper** on real council audio ([Schema Labs](https://schemalabs.substack.com/p/asr-finetuning)). Fine-tuning is worth it later, on your own meeting audio, not by downloading a community model.

**The biggest risk is Greek–English mixing.** In code-switched speech, Whisper's error rate is more than double its normal rate, and language pairs with different alphabets, like Greek–English, are hit hardest ([CS-FLEURS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.14161)). KIEFER measured **23.84% to 59.74% WER on code-switched Greek**, and two fine-tuning attempts made it worse ([arXiv 2609.13498](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13498)). Whisper has also been seen translating instead of transcribing in other language pairs ([Casablanca](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.04527)). On a Greek-heavy meeting with auto-detect, the text could come out in English. Two other documented failures need guarding against: invented subtitle text such as "Υπότιτλοι AUTHORWAVE" over music or silence ([arXiv 2406.15284](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.15284v1)), and **about 40% of overlapping speech simply dropped** ([arXiv 2609.13498](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13498)). The fix is a voice-activity filter (VAD) before Whisper, a no-speech threshold and a repetition penalty. Overlap has no fix in any of these models.

## Speed and live mode: no M1 Pro measurement exists, but the estimates leave headroom

The best measured runtime comparison put **mlx-whisper about 2x faster than whisper.cpp** on large-v3-turbo: 13.1 s vs 26.7 s on the same file. The hardware is unconfirmed, and whisper.cpp ran without explicit acceleration flags ([Bill Mill, Jan 2026](https://notes.billmill.org/dev_blog/2026/01/updated_my_mlx_whisper_vs._whisper.cpp_benchmark.html)). An older 2024 test on an actual 14" M1 Pro had whisper.cpp winning ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Aug/13/mlx-whisper)), so the ranking is not settled. A community benchmark on an M1 Max reports turbo at **about 14.3x real time** in batch mode ([vllm-mlx](https://vllm-mlx.is-a.dev/zh/benchmarks/audio/)). The M1 Pro has roughly half that GPU, so **estimate, not measured: 5–8x real time**. That is enough for live captions plus speaker labels on the same machine, and 32 GB of memory is not a constraint for any top-3 model.

For live text on screen, **WhisperLiveKit** (Apache-2.0) is the most complete open stack for Mac. It picks mlx-whisper automatically on macOS and offers two live decoding methods (SimulStreaming by default, LocalAgreement as an option). It also has Sortformer live speaker labels, a browser UI, a WebSocket endpoint and a native macOS client ([WhisperLiveKit](https://github.com/QuentinFuxa/WhisperLiveKit)). The WebSocket endpoint is a clean way to feed step 2 of your AI listener. Its language setting defaults to auto-detect, which "tends to bias toward English", so **pass `--language el` explicitly**. Its authors publish benchmarks only on an H100 data-centre GPU, and its Canary backend is **CPU-only on Apple Silicon**. That is why Canary stays #2: its live speed on your Mac is unknown and probably poor. **Estimate, not measured: 1–3 s delay** from speech to text for Whisper-class models on these live methods.

Parakeet's appeal is raw speed. FluidAudio runs it on Apple's Neural Engine at **~190x real time on an M4 Pro**, supports macOS 14, and is Apache-2.0 ([FluidAudio](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio)). Its true streaming model is English-only, though, so live Greek means the slower sliding-window mode, and you would have to write Swift. The native Swift alternative for Whisper, WhisperKit (MIT, macOS 14+), has free live mic streaming, but Argmax sells "real-time transcription with speakers" only in its paid Pro SDK ([WhisperKit](https://github.com/argmaxinc/WhisperKit)).

## Speaker labels work for Greek, but live labels cap at 4 speakers

Diarization (working out who spoke when) works on voice sound, not words. Greek should therefore not be a problem. This is an inference: **no source tested diarization on Greek audio**. The accuracy reference is **pyannote community-1**, with 7.2% diarization error (DER) on the VoxConverse test at 24x real time on an M4 Pro GPU ([speakrs README](https://docs.rs/crate/speakrs/latest/source/README.md)). On CPU it runs at only 1.5–2x ([FluidAudio benchmarks](https://docs.fluidinference.com/reference/benchmarks.md)). **Estimate, not measured: 8–15x real time on your M1 Pro**, which is fine after the meeting and tight for live use. Live options are weaker. WhisperLiveKit's Sortformer handles **1–4 speakers** and uses the NVIDIA Open Model License. FluidAudio's LS-EEND handles up to 10 speakers but needs Swift ([FluidAudio](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio)). The practical design is rough live labels during the meeting, then an accurate pyannote re-run afterwards.

A cheap trick removes half the diarization problem: **record your mic and the meeting app's audio as separate channels**, as the Boswell tool does with mic on left and system audio on right ([Boswell](https://github.com/rohandahale/boswell)). "Me vs. them" is then free, and diarization only has to split the remote side. To capture Teams/Zoom/Meet audio without extra drivers, macOS's Core Audio tap API needs **macOS 14.4 or later** ([AudioCap](https://github.com/insidegui/AudioCap); [Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coreaudio/capturing-system-audio-with-core-audio-taps.md)). Check the exact version under Apple menu > About This Mac. The fallback that works everywhere is the free BlackHole virtual audio device plus an "Aggregate Device" that combines it with your mic ([BlackHole wiki](https://github.com/ExistentialAudio/BlackHole/wiki/Use-BlackHole-as-a-Virtual-Microphone)).

Ready-made apps do not close the gap. **No app was found that documents local + live + speaker labels + Greek on macOS 14.** The closest are MacWhisper Pro, a paid one-time licence quoted at €59–$80 with live transcription and beta diarization ([getvoibe pricing](https://www.getvoibe.com/resources/macwhisper-pricing/)), and Meetily (MIT, free), which keeps speaker identification behind a $10/user/month PRO tier ([Meetily](https://github.com/Zackriya-Solutions/meeting-minutes)). Both run the same Whisper/Parakeet engines as above. They are also closed boxes for an AI-listener pipeline you want to build on.

## Cloud buys about 2 WER points on real Greek audio

Vendor Greek scores on FLEURS look far better than local ones. Speechmatics claims **6.58%** against Whisper's 12.32% on its own page ([Speechmatics](https://www.speechmatics.com/speech-to-text/greek)). ElevenLabs claims **4.2%** (3.1% in its headline, so the page contradicts itself) against Whisper's 13.8% ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/speech-to-text/greek)). Soniox reports 7.4% for itself in its own real-world YouTube study ([Soniox](https://soniox.com/speech-to-text/greek)). The only **independent** real-world Greek test tells a smaller story. On Greek municipal council recordings, **ElevenLabs Scribe v2 scored about 13% and Whisper about 15%** ([Schema Labs / OpenCouncil](https://schemalabs.substack.com/p/asr-finetuning)). That is one study, but it is the closest match to meeting audio: noisy rooms, overlap and names. A warning on sources: some Soniox comparison pages show "Greek" figures of 1.25–3.24% that match an **English** benchmark almost exactly. They are not Greek numbers ([Soniox benchmarks](https://soniox.com/benchmarks)).

If you need a cloud baseline, **Soniox stt-rt-v5** fits best. It confirms Greek live transcription and speaker labels, has an EU region where audio stays in region and is **not used for training**, and costs about **$0.12/hour live** ([Soniox](https://soniox.com/speech-to-text/greek)). Deepgram Nova-3, AWS Transcribe and Speechmatics also confirm Greek streaming ([Deepgram changelog](https://developers.deepgram.com/changelog/2026/7/21); [AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/transcribe/latest/dg/supported-languages.html); [Speechmatics](https://www.speechmatics.com/speech-to-text/greek)), and Speechmatics also offers on-prem deployment. ElevenLabs costs $0.40/hour ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/speech-to-text/greek)), but its Greek page names only Scribe v1 and does not confirm Greek for its real-time model. AssemblyAI's docs list Greek for batch only, despite marketing claims of Greek streaming ([AssemblyAI docs](https://www.assemblyai.com/docs/streaming/universal-streaming)). **No published Greek speaker-label accuracy exists for any cloud vendor.**

### The sources conflict on Mistral Voxtral and Greek

The notes disagree, and the disagreement is not fully resolved. Against Greek: Mistral's own Voxtral Transcribe 2 announcement names 13 strong languages, and **Greek is not one** ([Mistral news](https://mistral.ai/news/voxtral-transcribe-2)). NVIDIA's table marks the open Voxtral-Mini-3B-2507 as having no Greek ([Sekoyan et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14128v2)). Mistral's language docs name Greek only for text-to-speech, and give only language counts for the transcription models ([Mistral docs](https://docs.mistral.ai/studio/audio/specs-and-limits/languages)). For Greek: Mistral's **Voxtral 3** announcement (evaluation dated 2026-10-20) says it covers all 24 official EU languages and lists Greek ("EL") in its FLEURS evaluation set. It does not publish a Greek score, does not confirm Greek for the realtime model, and does not mention open weights ([Mistral Voxtral 3](https://mistral.ai/fr/news/voxtral-3/)). The most likely reading is that **Greek was added in the newest batch API (Voxtral 3) but is unconfirmed for live use and absent from the open, local weights.** For a local, live Greek listener, Voxtral is not a candidate today. Even in English, the open Voxtral realtime model was the weakest of 11 streaming systems in the Pipecat benchmark, at 4.44% WER ([Soniox benchmarks](https://soniox.com/benchmarks)).

## Conclusion

The real decision is not which model to pick but which Greek audio to test on. Published Greek numbers disagree by more than 2x for the same model, and vendor and independent tests disagree by 3x. Only a test on your own audio settles it. **My recommendation: record 30–60 minutes of a real meeting, correct the transcript by hand once, then score Whisper turbo, full large-v3, Canary-1B-v2 and Soniox against it.** This is the one step that turns every estimate in this report into a measured number for higher-ups, and it is the same corrected data you would need to fine-tune Whisper later. OpenCouncil is already doing that fine-tune with encouraging early results ([Schema Labs](https://schemalabs.substack.com/p/asr-finetuning)).

The weak points for step 1 are Greek–English mixing and overlapping speech, not raw speed or memory. No model in this report, local or cloud, has published evidence of handling either well. So the gain from extra effort is in the plumbing: forcing Greek, giving Whisper an `initial_prompt` with your team's English terms, filtering silence before transcription, and recording mic and meeting audio on separate channels. These are cheap and local, and they apply whichever model wins the test.
